\section{Introduction}

Valuing a gold producer under a single commodity-price path can conceal the model risk embedded in the commodity process itself. This paper is the publication-format endpoint of a broader eight-team research programme on Barrick Mining Corporation. The contributors came from different university pathways and therefore did not begin with the same exposure to measure-theoretic probability, econometrics, numerical simulation, software implementation, stochastic finance, and corporate valuation. For that reason, the project was first developed in a deliberately pedagogical, thesis-style form: definitions, derivations, diagnostics, implementation choices, empirical evidence and inter-module handoffs are made explicit rather than treated as common prior knowledge. The long-form version is intended to bridge these differences in background and provide a common technical language across the group. The present article takes the complementary editorial approach: it compresses that material into the methods and findings required to understand and audit the integrated valuation without reproducing the full teaching apparatus.

The eight source studies are therefore modular contributions, not eight replications of the same experiment. Teams 1--3 establish the mathematical, econometric and numerical foundations. \textbf{Team 1} develops the probability and pricing framework---measure and probability spaces, filtrations, Brownian motion, It\^o calculus, martingales, change of measure, GBM and Black--Scholes---that fixes the notation and risk-neutral conventions used downstream. \textbf{Team 2} provides the econometric language for inference and time-series analysis through OLS and robust inference, stationarity, AR/MA/ARIMA dynamics, rolling dependence and DCC-type correlation models. \textbf{Team 3} develops the Monte Carlo layer, covering random-number generation, distributional transforms, path discretisation, estimator error, variance reduction and non-Gaussian dependence tools. Together, these studies supply the common analytical and computational vocabulary on which the later empirical modules rely.

Teams 6--8 progressively move from these foundations to the market-implied gold-price layer. \textbf{Team 6} studies the limitations of constant-volatility continuous-path models and develops the jump and stochastic-volatility machinery---Poisson and compound-Poisson processes, L\'evy ideas, characteristic functions, Fourier pricing, CIR variance, and the Merton, Heston and Bates benchmarks. \textbf{Team 7} provides the long-horizon empirical context for gold and silver, examining returns, dependence, heavy tails, volatility persistence, GARCH dynamics and long-memory diagnostics rather than treating the stochastic specifications as purely theoretical choices. \textbf{Team 8} then turns this stack into the current option-implied experiment: Black--Scholes, Heston, Bates--Poisson and affine Full Bates--Hawkes are calibrated to a structured GLD option surface using constrained nonlinear optimisation, tested through residual diagnostics and rolling one-step-ahead validation, and finally used to generate risk-neutral gold-path distributions.

The corporate side is supplied by Teams 4 and 5. \textbf{Team 4} builds the operating bridge from commodity prices to Barrick's business through mine-level production and cost modelling: log-space ARIMA dynamics for the common cost trend, SARIMA-based production components, bounded recovery-rate simulation and the resulting operating-margin/EBITDA inputs. \textbf{Team 5} supplies the valuation bridge from operations to enterprise and equity value through FCFF/FCFE logic, WACC, ROIC--growth consistency, terminal-value closure, scenario analysis and stochastic DCF. In the final experiment, these two modules are deliberately frozen so that the effect of changing the gold-price engine can be identified rather than confounded with simultaneous changes in production, costs or corporate assumptions.

The resulting architecture assigns each variable and methodological claim to a source authority and permits cross-module handoffs only when date, unit, probability measure and accounting meaning are explicit. The research question is therefore narrow and testable: how much does Barrick's conditional equity-value distribution change when the gold-price layer is replaced by progressively richer stochastic specifications while the operating and corporate layers remain fixed? Model assessment combines in-sample option calibration with one-step-ahead tests against an expanding mean and the previous-day implied-volatility surface, separating structural richness from predictive dominance.
