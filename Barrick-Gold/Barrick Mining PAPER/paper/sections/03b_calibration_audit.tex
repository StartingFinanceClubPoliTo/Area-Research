\subsection{Why structured sampling is retained}
The option surface is irregular in strike and maturity. Team 8 therefore passes a structured Chebyshev subset to the optimizer rather than allowing densely listed regions to dominate the loss. The figure is the sampling audit for the same 24-node surface used by the current valuation, not a later diagnostic snapshot.

\begin{methodbox}[Evidence status]
The calibrated inputs, residual maps and valuation paths share the same 27 August Team 8 parameter set. The Treasury curve remains dated 24 July and the timing mismatch is retained as an explicit limitation.
\end{methodbox}

\subsection{What the residual maps add}
The residual heatmaps show where each specification systematically over- or under-prices the observed implied-volatility surface. Black--Scholes leaves the strongest smile and term-structure pattern; Heston reduces part of the curvature; jump-based specifications flatten the field further. Full Bates--Hawkes provides the lowest admitted IV RMSE, although its fitted branching ratio of 0.02 implies only mild self-excitation in this snapshot. The four maps are arranged as a two-by-two comparison so their common axes and colour scales remain directly comparable at a readable size.
