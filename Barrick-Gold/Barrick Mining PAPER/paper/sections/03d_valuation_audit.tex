\section{DCF Driver and Terminal-Value Audit}

Growth, reinvestment, WACC and terminal closure are shared across gold engines. Table~\ref{tab:dcf_inputs} summarizes the corporate assumptions.

\subsection{Growth, ROIC and reinvestment}
Growth and ROIC converge linearly to their stable levels over five years. Reinvestment follows $b_t=g_t/ROIC_t$, linking growth to the capital required to sustain it (Figure~\ref{fig:dcf_driver_schedule}).

\subsection{Common stochastic WACC}
WACC follows the bounded mean-reverting process in Equation~(17), with inputs in Table~\ref{tab:dcf_inputs}. Its annual innovation scale is $\sigma_w^*=\sigma_w\sqrt{(1-e^{-2\kappa_w})/(2\kappa_w)}=0.7308$ percentage points before clipping. All engines reuse 8,192 annual shock paths (Figure~\ref{fig:wacc_assumptions}).

\subsection{Terminal closure and sensitivity}
The terminal block uses $g_\infty=2.50\%$, $ROIC_\infty=10.50\%$ and terminal reinvestment of 23.81\%. At the current median terminal WACC of 8.384\%, the normalized terminal multiple is 13.27$\times$ final after-tax margin. The spread floor enforces $WACC_T>g_\infty$. No simulated rate reaches either clipping bound in the base run.

\begin{methodbox}[Interpretation]
These are methodological assumptions, not filing-calibrated Barrick estimates. Terminal closure dominates the reported operating proxy. The legacy positive terminal increases the mean by USD 1.14--1.49 billion relative to signed closure.
\end{methodbox}

The quarterly bridge multiplies simulated gold price less unit Cost of Sales, both in USD per ounce, by production in thousand ounces, then divides by 1,000 to obtain component margin in USD millions. Four quarterly margins are summed before annual taxes, reinvestment and discounting. This proxy is not reported EBIT or FCFF: production must be reconciled with sales and inventory, and depreciation, capital expenditure and working capital must enter without double counting. Fixed operating vectors exclude mine-level shocks and price-cost dependence.
