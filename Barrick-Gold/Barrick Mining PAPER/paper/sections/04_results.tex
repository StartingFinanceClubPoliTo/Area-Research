\subsection{Cross-model central tendency}
The refreshed 2 September run places all four conditional medians in a narrow USD~35.14--35.87 range. Their upper tails nevertheless remain broad: P90 values range from USD~101.43 to USD~104.72. With operations, WACC shocks and the DCF mapping fixed, these differences isolate the distributional effect of the option-implied gold engine.

Similar medians do not make the four stochastic laws interchangeable: the common corporate mapping can compress central differences while preserving asymmetric tails. The spread across simulated values is conditional economic dispersion, whereas Monte Carlo error concerns the numerical precision of an estimated mean or quantile. A fixed seed makes the calculation repeatable; it does not establish convergence as path count or time resolution changes. The plotted percentile ranges should therefore not be read as confidence intervals for a uniquely identified company value. At the extreme lower tail, negative proxy values expose the unreconciled corporate mapping rather than predict negative limited-liability share prices. Truncating them would conceal that limitation without repairing the accounting bridge.

\subsection{Why Full Bates--Hawkes is primary}
Full Bates--Hawkes is displayed as the primary structural sensitivity because it combines stochastic variance with self-exciting jumps and records the lowest date-equal rolling OOS IV RMSE. This choice is not an in-sample claim: Heston has the lowest current-snapshot IV RMSE. The fitted Hawkes branching ratio of 0.5569 satisfies $n<1$ and indicates a materially persistent, but stationary, self-excitation channel.
