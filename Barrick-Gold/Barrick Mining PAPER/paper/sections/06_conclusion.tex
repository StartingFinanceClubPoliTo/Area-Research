\section{Conclusion}

The experiment isolates the effect of stochastic gold dynamics while holding operations and corporate assumptions fixed. Black--Scholes, Heston and Full Bates--Hawkes retain broadly comparable medians, while Bates--Poisson produces a lower centre and wider upper tail. The completed market close lies above every conditional median, a directional result narrower than a claim of market mispricing.

Model quality is multidimensional. Full Bates--Hawkes provides the strongest current-surface fit, yet its fitted branching ratio is small and the rolling test does not establish universal forecasting superiority: none of the four models beats the previous-day IV benchmark. Team 4's updated operating layer uses Q1--Q2 2026 actuals and a separate Q3-onward forecast, but its uncertainty is frozen before model comparison.

The paper should therefore be read as a controlled attribution exercise. The newly explicit growth, WACC and terminal-value assumptions make the DCF auditable, but the terminal block still contributes roughly three quarters of positive enterprise value. Any fair-value interpretation would additionally require a reconciled commodity basis, physical-measure mapping, operating uncertainty and equity bridge.

\begin{findingbox}[Decision-relevant takeaway]
Under a common operating and DCF contract, changing the stochastic gold engine can affect both the central valuation signal and tail risk. The current architecture is informative for model comparison, but it is not yet a filing-reconciled fair-value model.
\end{findingbox}
